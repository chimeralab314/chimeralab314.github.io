excerpt: "Gradient descent is one of the most fundamental algorithms in numerical optimization and machine learning. Its underlying idea is deceptively simple: starting from an initial point, the algorithm iteratively moves in the direction opposite to the gradient of the objective function. Despite its simplicity, gradient descent exhibits rich mathematical behavior that depends on the geometry of the objective function, the choice of step size, and the properties of its Hessian matrix. Understanding these relationships provides valuable insight into convergence, computational efficiency, and the limitations of first-order optimization methods. In this article, we begin with a mathematical exploration of gradient descent on quadratic objective functions, using computational experiments to investigate how its convergence behavior changes with the dimension of the problem."
